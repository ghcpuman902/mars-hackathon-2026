Decision 1 · Site

Sunlight near the equator, or water at mid-latitude?

Jezero band, 18° NWarmest, best solar, closest to landing heritage. No shallow ice.
Mid-latitude ice, 35–45° NColder and darker, but buried ice within reach of a drill.

Life support water makeup is small, but propellant, construction and radiation shielding all want tonnes of water. A site with ice trades a few percent of solar for an open-ended water supply.

Decision 2 · Power

Can the settlement run on solar and batteries alone?

Solar + batteriesSimple, no nuclear licensing. Must ride out 81 sols of storm.
Fission baseload + solarReactor covers life support and heat; panels feed greenhouse and industry when the sky is clear.

The storm cuts panel output by roughly thirty times, not the four times the life-support table assumes. Storing 81 sols of demand would need more battery mass than the whole cargo manifest.

Decision 3 · Where to dig

Which ground do the builders work on?

Crater rim or rock fieldHigher, drier, better views. Twice the traversal energy and three times the stall risk.
Regolith plainLowest energy per metre and lowest hazard. Easy to trench, easy to drive.

Scouts run the terrain cost model and the swarm builders' battery margins show they cannot reach rough ground and return. The plain is chosen and charging waypoints are placed along the haul road.

Decision 4 · Depth

How deep does the settlement go?

Surface modules, 1 m coverFast. Still 57 mSv per Mars year and the full 58 °C daily swing.
Cut-and-cover, 3–5 mDose near Earth-like, temperature steady at the annual mean, still anchor-dependent.

Three constraints set the depth. Radiation wants 3 m. Thermal wants to be below the annual skin depth so the −100 °C nights never reach the liner. Structure says no amount of cover balances 70 kPa of cabin pressure, so anchors go in regardless, and deeper than 5 m only buys excavation cost.

Decision 5 · Volume and order

How much do we build before the crew arrives, and in what order?

The swarm can move about 27 m³ of regolith per sol at full success, so the volume budget is fixed by the sols available. Everything that keeps people alive is built first; the greenhouse shell is dug but not fitted out.

Comfort firstLarger private quarters, a commons, a surface viewing hall.
Power → shielded volume → water and O₂ → foodEach layer is complete before the next one starts.

Decision 6 · Exits and collectors

Which way do the doors and greenhouse collectors face?

Wind on Mars carries no structural load worth designing for. What it does is bury things. Every strong wind in the series blows toward the north-east, so that is where dunes form and that is where nothing important goes.

Doors on the lee sideSheltered from gusts, but sitting exactly where drift accumulates.
Doors and collectors upwind, fences downwindAirlocks face south-west; sacrificial dust fences and a dune trap sit north-east.

Decision 7 · Storm rule

How much warning do we get, and what shuts down?

The dust goes from clear to opaque in a single sol. There is no ramp to forecast, so the rule has to be a trip: the moment optical depth crosses 1.0, the settlement is already in storm mode. Robots park on charging pads, heating switches to reactor waste heat, and every non-essential load drops.

Forecast-led shutdownNeeds a lead time the data does not show.
Zero-warning tripStorm mode is a state, entered on a threshold, exited after 10 clear sols.

What do 100 people actually consume, and what has to be made here?

Decision 9 · Daylight

How does sunlight reach people and plants living 4 m underground?

Lighting the greenhouse with LEDs fed by panels would cost more power than everything else on the settlement combined. Piping the light down loses less on the way, so most of the crop area sits under fibre light pipes with LEDs only as storm backup. People get a surface sunroom behind half a metre of water, which shields as well as regolith and can be looked through.

PV to LEDRoughly 10 % of collected sunlight reaches the leaf. 0.9 MW of demand.
Fibre light pipes + water-shielded sunroom30–50 % of collected light delivered; collectors need dust cleaning.

Decision 10 · Living through it

What changes inside when the sky goes dark for 81 sols?

The storm mode proven by the robots now has occupants. Cabin air is the first thing to drift: ventilation rises, recovery efficiency drops, and water makeup nearly doubles. Surface trips stop. Greenhouse lighting drops to the seed-stock minimum and the reactor carries the rest.

Keep normal operationsRover risk and water makeup climb with no resupply behind them.
Seal, shed, and waitEVA suspended, greenhouse on minimum light, CO₂ scrubbing at full rate.

What Earth taught us that Mars throws out

The same log, read as a comparison. Left is the reflex we bring from Earth; right is what the numbers above forced instead.

Does not carry over

  • Wind loading. Earth codes size walls for hundreds of pascals. Mars peaks under 5 Pa. Dust deposition replaces wind load as the design driver.
  • Gravity holds buildings down. Here 70 kPa of cabin pressure pushes up, and 3 m of cover supplies 18 kPa. Every habitat is an anchored pressure vessel in tension.
  • Windows are free. Every square metre of daylight costs shielding or power. Light is piped, not framed.
  • Fire rating by wall thickness. A 160 mm two-hour wall means nothing. Fire is controlled by oxygen partial pressure.
  • Concrete. Needs water the loop cannot spare. Sintered regolith, sulfur concrete and basalt fibre take its place.
  • Cooling by air. No convection outside. Waste heat leaves by radiators.

Mars does better, and Earth could copy

  • Closed loops by default. 94 % water and 91 % oxygen recovery are the entry requirement, not a target.
  • Heat and power as one system. Reactor waste heat is the district heating from the first module.
  • Robots finish before people arrive. No one lives next to a construction site.
  • Deterministic siting. One wind rose, one sun path, one predictable storm season. Orientation is a calculation, not a compromise.
  • Compact by necessity. Shared volume underground beats sprawl, and the walk to anything is under 200 m.
  • Build order by survival value. Power, shelter, water, food, then leisure. The hotel is last on purpose.
